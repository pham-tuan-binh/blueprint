2Tools you need

  • MIG welder
  • Hammer drill and masonry bit for the anchors
  • Chop saw or angle grinder with cut-off discs
  • Angle grinder with flap disc
  • Welding table or flat floor
  • Square, tape, scriber, spirit level
  • Six or more clamps
  • Drill, 13 mm bit, M12 tap
  • A spacer block for the bar gaps

3Coordinates

Every position is from the datum: x from the left end of the top, y from the front edge, z up from the floor.

  1. Mark the datum lines on your table or floor.
  2. Measure x along one, then y square to it.
  3. Mark each point, then set the parts on the marks.

Dimensions marked ℄ are centre to centre.

4Jig drawings

Each step's jig shows the parts to fit now in dark, the parts already there in light, and every joint labelled. The joint table under it gives the same points as numbers.

5Squareness

Not equal: push the long diagonal until they match, then check again.

6Buying stock

The materials list says how many lengths to buy; the cutting plan shows which pieces come out of each. Reject bent or damaged lengths. Write the piece mark on every piece as you cut it.

7Cut sheets

  1. Measure the long point from a square end.
  2. Mark it all the way round.
  3. Cut on the waste side. Deburr.

x4 = cut four the same. Long point = overall length. Each end says how it is cut and what it meets.

8Joint types

flush: stops level with the other member's outside. butt: cut square, sits flat on the other member's face.

9Tacking and welding

  1. Weld two offcuts and break them in a vise first: the metal should tear, not the weld.
  2. Tack each joint on two faces. Measure. Wrong: break the tack and redo.
  3. Weld out in short runs, alternating sides, clamped until cold.
  4. Measure again.

10The checks

Every size the fields allow is checked before you see it: every joint can be made, nothing floats or clashes, pieces fit the stock, and it holds its loads. A safety factor of 2 means it would take twice the load before the metal bends for good. Sag is how far it moves under the rated load.

A plan is a starting point, not an engineer's sign-off. Load-test it gently.

11Tolerances

Cut ±1 mm, tacked ±1 mm, finished ±2 mm. Out of tolerance: fix it now, before the next step.

12Safety

  • Eye protection for every cut. Ear protection for saws and grinders.
  • Welding: auto-darkening helmet, leather gloves, cotton sleeves. Never weld galvanised steel. Extinguisher in reach.
  • Load-test gently before you trust it.

13Glossary

datumDatum: the zero every position is measured from. The legend says where x, y and z start.
joint positionsWhere the members' centre lines meet, measured from the datum. Mark each one before you place the parts.
℄℄ = a centre-line dimension: from the middle of one member to the middle of the other, not outside to outside.
jigThis step's parts, full layout: dark = fit now, light = already there. Set the parts on the marked points.
plumbExactly vertical. Check two faces with a level.
diagMeasure corner to corner both ways. Equal = square. Not equal: push the long diagonal until they match.
checkA gate: pass it before the next step. Fixing it later costs far more.
warnA warning: read it before you start the step.
markPiece mark: one letter per different piece. Write it on each piece as you cut it.
long pointLong point: the overall length of the piece, tip to tip.
marksDistances from the square end of the stock to the cut, one per edge. Mark every edge, join the marks, cut.
sheetsOne cut sheet per piece mark: its length and how each end is cut.
deburrTake the sharp edge off with a file or a deburring tool.
mitreTwo members of the same size cut at the same angle so they meet on the middle line, like a picture frame.
buttThis end is cut square and sits flat against the face of the other member.
flushWhere sizes differ, the end stops level with the outside of the other member.
fittingThe pipe goes into a bought fitting (a tee, an elbow, a cap) until it bottoms out in the socket.
tackA small spot of weld that just holds parts in place. Easy to break and redo, unlike a full weld.
weld outWelding every joint all the way round, in short runs spread around the frame so the heat does not pull it out of shape.
cementSolvent cement melts the pipe and fitting together. Primer first, then cement, push home with a quarter turn. It sets in seconds.
dry fitPut everything together without glue first, so a mistake costs nothing.
primerPrimer cleans and softens the pipe and the socket so the cement bites. Brush it on both, then cement while it is still wet.
cureCement sets in seconds but takes hours to reach full strength. Handle the frame gently after about 2 hours; load it only after the full cure time on the can.
chamferA small bevel on the outside edge of the cut end, so it slides into the socket without scraping off the cement.
printPrint the part with the settings shown, the way it says to lay it on the bed. The .stl is the part at this size.
stockBought lengths. The cutting plan shows which pieces come out of which length.
safety factorSafety factor: how many times the load it could take before the metal starts to bend for good. 2 or more for furniture, 3 for anything a person stands on.
sagHow far the loaded part moves under the rated load. Small enough that you will not notice it.
tol± is how far off you may be and still be right.
load testLoad it a little at a time up to the rated load, watching for movement, before you trust it.
